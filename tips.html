FIRST PERSON BOXING TIPS

How to win rounds with your jab, save your stamina and make your opponent miss first

🥊 THE JAB IS THE WHOLE FIGHT

The jab is the most important punch in the game. It is fast, it costs almost nothing, it sets up every heavier shot you own, and it stops opponents from walking straight into your combinations. If you only train one thing, train this.

Lead With It

Every string should begin with a jab. The Cross and the body shots do far more damage when they land on a fighter who already flinched at the first punch.

Throw It Twice

A second jab after the opponent blocks the first keeps the guard tied up and opens the line to the body. Jab-Jab-Cross is a beginner staple for a reason.

Use It to Reset

When you are cornered or low on stamina, a jab buys you two seconds of distance and a chance to breathe. It is also your best safe answer to a charging opponent.

⚡ MANAGING STAMINA

Stamina decides how the second half of a fight goes. Fighters who burn everything in round one spend rounds two and three getting hit without answering, and that is how a good opponent wins on the cards.

Punch Stamina Cost Best Moment Avoid
Jab Minimal Every exchange, any time Never
Cross Low Round 1 and round 3 openings Emptying it in the last 20 seconds
Hooks Medium After a jab, at close range Throwing them while swinging wildly
Body shots Medium Early rounds, when they are fresh Piling on when the opponent is already hurt
Heavy flurries High One committed burst per round Chaining them for 30 seconds
  • • Three-round plan: work in round one, take the body in round two, hunt the finish in round three when the legs go.
  • • Never chase a hurt opponent with everything: throw, step out, reset with a jab.
  • • Corner yourself: the ring ropes are fine, but backing into a corner with an opponent throwing is how rounds get stolen.
  • • Rest two days before a title fight: a fresh fighter wins in the third round.

🛡️ DEFENSE AND READING

Defense is a scoring system of its own. Every punch you avoid is damage you do not need to recover from, and the heavy punchers on the roster all win by landing the one shot that matters.

The Check Hook

The cheapest way to break an opponent's rhythm. Throw it as they enter range, then move. It denies them the shot without you committing to anything.

Footwork Beats Power

Moving out of the pocket makes even "Steel Fist" Thompson miss. Walk him down slowly, do not run him down, and keep him swinging at air.

Block the Body Early

Body damage compounds. Fighters like "El Gato" Martinez live on the body, so guard low and take the fight to the head when you can.

Respect the Right Hand

"The Hammer" Silva has a 12-0 record with 10 knockouts and his left hook is the one to watch. Do not walk into it while you are enjoying your own success.

🔍 READING OPPONENTS

Opponent Style Their Threat Your Answer
"Lightning" Leo Blinding hand speed Fast counters, hard to catch Jab-and-move, do not stand still
"Iron Eagle" Marcus Technical counterpuncher Waits for you to overcommit Be patient, punish his own counters

🏋️ TRAINING PRIORITIES

Skill points are limited, so spend them in this order. The order matters more than the amount: a fighter with a great jab and poor defense will beat a fighter with the reverse.

  1. Jab. The foundation. Everything else lands because of it.
  2. Footwork. Defense and stamina both scale with movement.
  3. Defense. Blocking and rolling stop the knockouts you would otherwise eat.
  4. Power. Last, because power without accuracy is wasted points.

Between Fights

Heavy bag work sharpens timing, footwork drills fix where you are standing, and recovery training after a loss matters more than more bag work. Do not skip recovery.

Before a Title Fight

Rest two full days. Take a sparring session that ends while you still feel good. Do not add new techniques the week of the fight.

❌ MISTAKES THAT COST FIGHTS

  • Swinging for the fences: power punches thrown out of range score nothing and drain your stamina.
  • Standing in front of a KO artist: move sideways, even if it costs you the clean shot.
  • Chasing after a knockdown: the referee will let it happen, but the counter will be there when you get up.
  • Loading up the same string: opponents read your patterns. Vary the entry punch.
  • Forgetting to block the body: body shots do not knock you out, they make you drop your hands.
  • Training power before jab: the return on early power points is small and the cost is real.
