FIRST PERSON BOXING BEGINNER GUIDE

Your first ten bouts, explained - how to train, how to fight and how to survive the amateur card

① UNDERSTAND THE CAREER STRUCTURE

The career runs in a straight line, and knowing the shape of it stops you from wasting skill points in the amateur ring where they do nothing for later.

Stage What Happens Fights Focus
Amateur Career Ten local bouts to earn your pro card 10 Learn the controls, build basics
Pro Card Your first professional contract - Commit to a weight class
Strawweight Five fights, ending with a title eliminator 5 Survive Garcia, then move up
Title Divisions Flyweight through welterweight Varies Win a belt in each class
World Champion The final climb, then Legend Mode - Peak conditioning, best defense

② THE FIRST FIVE THINGS TO LEARN

1. The Jab

Your fastest punch and your best tool. It costs almost no stamina, it sets up everything else and it keeps opponents honest.

2. Blocking and Rolling

Blocking stops punches. Rolling under them moves your head out of the way. Between them they stop the knockouts that end amateur careers early.

3. Footwork

Moving makes you harder to hit and less tired. If you only learn one non-punch skill, learn to move your feet.

4. Two Reliable Combos

Learn Jab-Cross and Jab-Jab-Cross, plus a body shot string. Three strings you can execute without thinking beat twelve you cannot remember.

5. Managing Stamina

Watch the stamina bar early. If it is low in round one, you are throwing too much, and the fix is the jab and the footwork, not more punches.

6. Reading Your Opponent

Every fighter has a pattern. Watch their first two exchanges, find the punch they reach for, and start blocking that one.

③ SURVIVING THE AMATEUR BOUTS

Ten amateur bouts is a long time to spend in the gym. Treat it as training with the volume turned down rather than as ten fights you need to win.

  • Win by decision, not by heroics. Points decide most amateur bouts. Clean, accurate punches over ten rounds of chaos.
  • Learn the clock. If the game shows a round timer, use it to plan. Busy in round one, sharp in round three.
  • Take the knockdown lesson. If you get dropped, note which punch landed and block it next time.
  • Train after every bout. Each skill point is a small permanent gain. Put them in jab, footwork and defense first.
  • Do not chase knockouts. A KO is nice, but a lost bout loses your ranking progress.
  • Keep the streak alive. Amateur consistency is what gets you on a pro card.

④ YOUR FIRST PRO FIGHT

Your pro card starts at Strawweight with five fights ending in a title eliminator. The opponents get better fast, so plan the run rather than reacting to it.

Opponent Style Threat Level Plan
"The Hammer" Silva 12-0 with 10 knockouts High Watch the left hook, move off the line
"Knuckles" Chen 15-2, former amateur champion Medium Experienced, so counter his patterns
"El Gato" Martinez 10-0, body-first fighter Medium Guard the body early, take the fight upstairs
"El Toro" Garcia Aggressive champion Medium Weather the flurry, win rounds 2 and 3
Title Eliminator Win this and you get the belt shot High Rest two days, fight sharp not wild

⑤ TRAINING ROUTINE BETWEEN FIGHTS

The training menu looks simple, but the order you use it in decides how fast your fighter improves.

Heavy Bag

Sharpens timing and combinations. Put it first in the week so the work is fresh.

Footwork Drills

Keeps your stamina up and stops you standing still in front of a counterpuncher.

Recovery

Do this after a loss. Coming back tired from a bad fight is how careers stall.

Skill point order: jab, then footwork, then defense, then power. Save power for last because it only pays off once your accuracy and movement are already in place.

❌ BEGINNER MISTAKES

  • Spending every point on power: a powerful fighter with a slow jab loses to a fast one.
  • Training hard the day before a fight: rest two days instead. Fresh legs win round three.
  • Standing still after a combo: the pause is where the counter comes from.
  • Ignoring the body: body shots sap stamina and drop the guard for the head.
  • Learning fifteen combos at once: three reliable strings beat fifteen forgotten ones.
  • Panicking after a knockdown: reset, guard up, take the win on the cards.

✓ YOUR FIRST MILESTONES

  • ☐ Land your first clean jab-cross combination
  • ☐ Finish a full bout without throwing yourself out of stamina
  • ☐ Win your first five amateur bouts
  • ☐ Put your first skill points into jab and footwork
  • ☐ Complete the amateur card and get the pro contract
  • ☐ Beat Silva and Chen in your first pro run
  • ☐ Win the Strawweight title eliminator
  • ☐ Learn to defend the body against Martinez
  • ☐ Rest two full days before your next title fight
